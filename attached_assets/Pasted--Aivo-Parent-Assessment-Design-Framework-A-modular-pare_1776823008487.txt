# Aivo Parent Assessment — Design Framework

A modular parent assessment designed to generate a baseline profile of a child across cognitive, communication, social-emotional, sensory, motor, adaptive, and interest/strength domains. Built to feed a downstream "child baseline" used for personalization, goal-setting, and progress tracking.

---

## 1. Design Principles

These should drive every question-level decision:

1. **Strengths-first, not deficit-first.** Parents are more engaged, more honest, and more accurate when the form doesn't feel like a clinical evaluation. Open with interests and strengths before concerns.
2. **Parent-friendly language.** No jargon. Not "receptive language," but "understanding what others say." Not "executive function," but "planning and finishing tasks."
3. **Observable behavior only.** Ask what the parent *sees*, not what they think it *means*. "How often does your child follow a 2-step instruction?" — not "Does your child have working memory issues?"
4. **Concrete frequency, not abstract severity.** Replace "mild/moderate/severe" with "Never / Rarely / Sometimes / Often / Always" or specific frequencies ("fewer than 1×/week," "daily").
5. **Age-banded branching.** One assessment cannot cover a 3-year-old and a 14-year-old. Use age bands to drive which items appear.
6. **Chunked and resumable.** Target ~20–30 minutes total, split into 5–7 sections the parent can complete across sittings. Autosave always.
7. **Explain the "why."** A one-line rationale above each section ("This helps us understand how your child communicates so we can match content to their level") dramatically improves completion and data quality.
8. **Culturally inclusive.** Don't assume two-parent, English-first, or neurotypical family norms. Allow "caregiver" framing, multilingual environments, and varied family structures.
9. **Dignity for the child.** Parents often share this screen with their child. Avoid language a child would find hurtful if they read it.

---

## 2. Assessment Structure Overview

A 7-section modular design. Sections can be reordered or made optional based on your platform's focus.

| # | Section | Time | Purpose |
|---|---------|------|---------|
| 0 | Welcome & Consent | 1 min | Set expectations, get consent, explain data use |
| 1 | Child Basics & Family Context | 2 min | Demographics, language environment, household |
| 2 | Strengths, Interests & Motivations | 3–4 min | Positive anchor; informs content personalization |
| 3 | Developmental & Medical History | 3–5 min | Milestones, health, prior services |
| 4 | Current Functioning (core domains) | 10–15 min | The bulk of the baseline data |
| 5 | Learning Style & School Context | 3 min | How they learn, school setting |
| 6 | Parent Priorities & Goals | 2–3 min | What matters most to this family right now |
| 7 | Wrap-up & Next Steps | 1 min | Summary, confirmation, what happens next |

**Section 4 ("Current Functioning") is the core.** It contains sub-modules for each domain — Communication, Social-Emotional, Behavior & Regulation, Sensory, Motor, Adaptive/Daily Living, and Cognitive/Academic. Which sub-modules are required vs. optional depends on your platform focus (see Section 8 of this doc).

## 3. Section-by-Section Design

### Section 0 — Welcome & Consent

Single screen. Three elements:
- A warm 2–3 sentence intro from aivo in plain language.
- Estimated time ("About 25 minutes — you can save and come back anytime").
- Consent checkboxes: data use, storage, who sees results, ability to delete.

### Section 1 — Child Basics & Family Context

Keep this short and functional. Examples:

- Child's first name (or nickname they prefer)
- Date of birth (drives age-band branching)
- Pronouns / gender (optional, inclusive options)
- Who is completing this? (parent, grandparent, foster parent, other caregiver — free text allowed)
- Languages spoken in the home (multi-select + primary)
- Who lives with the child? (structured but flexible)
- Any siblings? Ages? (useful context, not required)

### Section 2 — Strengths, Interests & Motivations

This section should feel *fun* for the parent. It's also where you get your richest personalization data.

Sample items:
- "What are 3 things your child loves to do?" (open text, chips)
- "What topics, characters, or activities can they talk about endlessly?" (open text)
- "What makes your child laugh?" (open text)
- "What are they naturally good at?" (multi-select: art, music, memory, puzzles, sports, numbers, storytelling, building, caring for others, animals, noticing details, comforting others, negotiating, inventing games, other — and free text)
- "How does your child best receive praise?" (single-select: words, hugs/physical, treats, alone time, shared activity, earned privilege)
- "What usually motivates them to try something hard?" (multi-select)

**Baseline outputs from this section:** interest tags for content matching, reinforcement style, strength domains.

### Section 3 — Developmental & Medical History

Ask only what you'll actually use. Recommendations:

- Age-banded milestone questions (only show relevant to child's age):
  - Under 5: first words, walking, toilet training, pointing/gestures
  - 5–8: reading onset, forming sentences with peers, fine motor (drawing, scissors)
  - 9+: these are usually not useful — replace with "were there any developmental concerns in early childhood?" single question
- Any diagnoses? (multi-select with "prefer not to say" and free-text "other"; include categories like ADHD, autism, learning differences, anxiety, depression, speech/language, hearing, vision, chronic medical, none)
- Any current therapies or services? (OT, PT, speech, ABA, counseling, tutoring, IEP/504, none, other)
- Any medications that affect attention, mood, or learning? (yes/no, optional details)
- Sleep — hours per night, quality
- Vision & hearing — last checked, any known issues

**Design note:** Make diagnosis questions explicitly optional. Many parents skip or abandon assessments when forced to disclose.

### Section 4 — Current Functioning

This is where most of the baseline is built. Organized as sub-modules. Use a consistent 5-point frequency scale throughout: **Never / Rarely / Sometimes / Often / Always** plus "Not sure / Haven't observed."

#### 4a. Communication & Language

- Understands simple instructions (age-appropriate examples given inline)
- Understands multi-step instructions
- Uses full sentences to express needs
- Tells you about something that happened earlier in the day
- Has back-and-forth conversations (3+ turns)
- Asks questions to learn new things
- Understands jokes, sarcasm, or figures of speech (for older children)
- Reads body language and facial expressions
- Open: "Is there anything specific about how your child communicates we should know?"

#### 4b. Social-Emotional

- Seeks out other children to play with
- Plays cooperatively (takes turns, shares)
- Has one or more close friendships
- Recovers from disappointment within a reasonable time
- Can name what they're feeling
- Shows empathy when someone else is upset
- Handles transitions between activities
- Copes with losing a game or being told "no"
- Open: "What are your biggest social or emotional concerns right now, if any?"

#### 4c. Behavior & Regulation

- Follows rules at home
- Follows rules at school
- Waits their turn
- Finishes tasks they start
- Can sit and focus for [age-appropriate window]
- Has meltdowns or big emotional reactions
- Becomes aggressive (hits, kicks, throws) when upset
- Hurts themselves when upset
- Open: "What helps your child calm down when they're upset?"
- Open: "What seems to trigger big reactions?"

*Note: Self-harm and aggression items should trigger a soft alert in your system — not punitive, but routed for human review + resource suggestions.*

#### 4d. Sensory Profile

- Covers ears, hides from, or gets upset by loud or sudden sounds
- Seems bothered by lights, screens, or visually busy places
- Avoids certain textures (clothing tags, foods, surfaces)
- Seeks out movement (spinning, jumping, rocking)
- Seeks out deep pressure (tight hugs, weighted things)
- Has a strong reaction to smells or tastes
- Notices small details most people miss
- Open: "Are there sensory things that calm or dysregulate your child?"

#### 4e. Motor

- Fine motor (age-banded): holds pencil, uses scissors, buttons, ties shoes, types
- Gross motor: runs, jumps, climbs, catches, rides bike, balance
- Handwriting legibility (for school-age)
- Stamina for physical activity
- Open: "Any motor skills that are harder for your child than peers?"

#### 4f. Adaptive / Daily Living

Age-banded. Examples for ages 5–10:
- Dresses self
- Manages bathroom needs independently
- Brushes teeth independently
- Eats a variety of foods
- Packs their own bag / remembers belongings
- Tells time / manages simple schedules
- Handles small amounts of money
- Knows personal info (name, phone, address) in case of emergency

#### 4g. Cognitive / Academic

This overlaps with school context (Section 5). Keep it focused on *how they think*, not grade-level performance (which parents often don't know accurately):
- Curious — asks lots of questions
- Remembers things from a long time ago
- Notices patterns
- Problem-solves when things don't go as expected
- Sticks with tasks that are hard
- Pretends, imagines, tells stories
- Understands cause and effect
- Open: "When has your child surprised you with how smart they are?"

### Section 5 — Learning Style & School Context

- Current school setting (public, private, homeschool, microschool, not in school, other)
- Grade level
- Currently has IEP / 504 / ILP? (with opt-in to upload document)
- How your child learns best (multi-select: watching, doing, reading, listening, talking it through, teaching someone else, moving while learning, being alone, working with others)
- Attention span for non-preferred tasks (age-banded minutes)
- Attention span for preferred tasks
- What time of day are they sharpest?
- Open: "What's a recent learning win we should know about?"
- Open: "Where does learning break down for them?"

### Section 6 — Parent Priorities & Goals

End with this — not the start. It hits different after a parent has reflected on their child for 20 minutes.

- "If aivo could help with ONE thing over the next 3 months, what would it be?" (open)
- Rank top 3 priorities (drag-and-drop): academic skills, confidence, social skills, independence, emotional regulation, communication, motor skills, focus/attention, creativity, specific interest exploration, family harmony, other
- "What does success look like for your child in the next year?" (open)
- "What do you worry about?" (open, optional)
- "What are you hopeful about?" (open)

### Section 7 — Wrap-up & Next Steps

- Summary of what was submitted (plain-language recap)
- What aivo will do with it (generate baseline, suggest starting points, etc.)
- When they'll see results
- Invitation to add anything they feel was missed (one final open box — consistently the highest-signal field in assessments like this)
- Option to invite a co-parent or teacher to add their perspective

## 4. Question Format Guidelines

| Format | When to use |
|--------|-------------|
| 5-point frequency Likert | Any behavior/skill you're measuring across a population |
| Multi-select with "other" text | Interests, strengths, diagnoses, supports |
| Single-select | Mutually exclusive attributes (setting, primary language) |
| Rank / drag-and-drop | Priorities, preferences |
| Age-banded yes/no | Developmental milestones |
| Sliders | Avoid for clinical items — parents anchor differently; use discrete scales |
| Open text (short) | One per sub-section — captures what structured items miss |
| Open text (long) | Only at end of major sections; always optional |

Every Likert item should include a **"Not sure / Haven't observed"** option. Forcing parents to guess degrades data more than missing values do.

## 5. Generating the Baseline from Responses

The assessment should feed a **multi-domain baseline profile** — not a single score. Suggested output structure:

```
Baseline Profile
├── Interest Graph          (tags + weights from Section 2)
├── Communication Level     (composite from 4a, age-normed)
├── Social-Emotional Level  (composite from 4b)
├── Regulation Profile      (from 4c + 4d: strengths, triggers, supports)
├── Sensory Profile         (from 4d: seeker/avoider patterns)
├── Motor Profile           (from 4e)
├── Adaptive Level          (from 4f, age-normed)
├── Cognitive/Learning Profile (from 4g + 5)
├── Environmental Context   (from 1, 3, 5)
├── Flags for Human Review  (safety items from 4c, specific concerns)
└── Priority Goals          (from 6, top 3 ranked)
```

**Scoring approach for Likert composites:**
- Map Never→0, Rarely→1, Sometimes→2, Often→3, Always→4
- Within each sub-domain, average relevant items (reverse-coding concern-framed items)
- Convert to a 1–5 level *for that age band* (age-normed, not raw)
- Never surface a number to the parent — translate to plain language ("Kai's communication profile suggests they're comfortable with back-and-forth conversation but may benefit from support on multi-step instructions.")

**Handling "Not sure":**
- Exclude from composite
- If >30% of a sub-domain is "Not sure," mark that domain as low-confidence in the baseline and avoid making strong recommendations from it.

**Handling safety flags (self-harm, aggression):**
- Do NOT score numerically — always route to a human review queue
- Surface resources to the parent immediately (not after review)

## 6. Parent Experience (UX) Requirements

These make or break completion rates:

- **Progress bar** with section titles, not just percentage
- **Autosave** on every field blur
- **Resume link** emailed after first drop-off (not just in-app)
- **Time estimates** per section, not just total
- **Skip buttons** on every optional section, with a small prompt ("You can always add this later")
- **Co-parent invite** — send a link for a second caregiver to complete their own version; compare perspectives
- **Mobile-first** — the majority of completions will happen on phones, often in waiting rooms
- **Plain-language preview** of what the baseline will look like *before* they start, so they know what they're getting
- **Edit-after-submit** — parents' view of their child changes; let them revisit and update

## 7. Implementation Notes

### Branching logic
- **Age band** is the most important branch. Define 4 bands: 2–4, 5–8, 9–12, 13+. Each sub-module has a different item set per band.
- **Diagnosis-aware branching** (optional): if a parent indicates autism, ADHD, speech/language, etc., surface a few diagnosis-specific follow-ups — but don't require them.
- **Concern-driven branching**: if a parent marks "Often" or "Always" on a concern item, ask one follow-up ("When this happens, what usually helps?") rather than many.

### Validation
- No hard required fields except DOB and consent. Everything else is soft-required with a "I'd rather not say" escape.
- Validate sanity (DOB is not in the future, age matches milestone items), not completeness.

### Internationalization
- Write items in English first but avoid idioms.
- Flag items that reference culturally specific norms (eye contact, sleep arrangements, independence expectations) for localization review.

### Data ethics
- Be explicit about: who sees this, how long it's stored, whether it's used for model training, how to delete.
- Parents should be able to export their responses as a PDF for their own records or to share with a clinician.

## 8. Configuring for Your Platform Focus

The sub-modules in Section 4 should be weighted differently depending on what aivo is primarily for. A few common configurations:

- **General K–12 learning platform:** emphasize 4a (communication), 4g (cognitive), Section 5 (learning style). Make 4d (sensory) and 4f (adaptive) optional.
- **Neurodivergent-focused platform:** all sub-modules of Section 4 are required; add diagnosis-specific branching in Section 3.
- **Special education / IEP support:** add an IEP/504 document upload in Section 5; expand 4f (adaptive) and add present-levels alignment.
- **Early childhood (ages 2–6):** heavily weight milestones (Section 3), motor (4e), and adaptive (4f); reduce 4g (cognitive) to observational items only.
- **Social-emotional / mental health focus:** expand 4b and 4c; add a validated short screen (e.g., adapted PSC-17 items) with appropriate licensing.

## 9. What I'd Build First (MVP Cut)

If I were shipping v1 tomorrow, I'd cut to:

1. Sections 0, 1, 2, 6, 7 in full (framing, context, strengths, goals, wrap-up)
2. Section 3: diagnoses + services only (skip milestones)
3. Section 4: only 4a (communication), 4b (social-emotional), 4g (cognitive)
4. Section 5: just the learning style multi-select + one open text

Target: 12–15 minutes. Then layer in sensory, motor, adaptive, and branching logic in v2 once you see completion data.

---

## 10. Open Design Questions for Your Team

Worth deciding before build:

1. Are you collecting this once (baseline) or recurring (monthly/quarterly re-assessment)? The design changes meaningfully for longitudinal tracking.
2. Will there be a corresponding child-facing assessment, or teacher version? If yes, design for triangulation.
3. What's your position on surfacing "levels" or "scores" to parents? Strong recommendation: translate everything to plain language, never show a raw number.
4. How will you handle co-parents with different answers? Store both, flag disagreements for the baseline generator?
5. What happens when answers suggest a need aivo can't meet (e.g., acute mental health)? Have a referral pathway ready before launch.
