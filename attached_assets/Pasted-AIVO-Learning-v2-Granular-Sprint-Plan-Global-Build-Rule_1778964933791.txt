AIVO Learning v2 — Granular Sprint Plan
Global Build Rules for Every Sprint

Every sprint must satisfy these rules before it is accepted:

No dead buttons.
No placeholder routes.
No primary route returns 404.
Every form has validation.
Every API response uses the BFF success/failure shape.
Every user-facing error has a retry or recovery path.
Every route has loading, empty, error, and success states.
Every protected route checks role, tenantId, and learner scope.
Every new feature includes unit, integration, and Playwright coverage.
Every learner-facing screen supports keyboard navigation and screen-reader labels.
Every AI-generated object is validated before being persisted.

Recommended stack:

Frontend:
Next.js App Router
React

AI:

That said, a few things I'd still tighten before calling it complete:

1. **Sprint ordering has a load-bearing dependency problem.** Sprint 26 (curriculum/skill graph) is a prerequisite for Sprints 8 (baseline), 9 (mastery), 11 (lesson generation), and 16 (quests) — the release gate even says "no baseline question without curriculum alignment." Building those sprints first against seeded stub skills means rewriting them after Sprint 26 lands. Same with Sprint 24 (real auth) — every BFF guard, audit log, and consent check in Sprints 4–23 assumes an identity model that doesn't exist yet. Move 24 and 26 earlier (right after Sprint 3) or accept that Sprints 4–23 are a throwaway prototype.

2. **Sprint 27 (AI safety) should also shift left.** It's gating Sprints 8, 11, 13, and 17, which all generate or accept AI content. Building the validator/moderation pipeline alongside the first AI sprint is far cheaper than retrofitting safety into eight already-shipped features.

3. **Security review is still missing as a discrete sprint.** Pen testing is mentioned in passing in "Final Sprint" but there's no scope for: SOC 2 readiness, threat modeling, secrets management, key rotation, dependency/SBOM scanning, vulnerability disclosure policy, or incident response runbooks. For a platform holding IEPs and child data, schools will ask for SOC 2 Type II and a signed DPA before signing — that's a multi-month effort, not a checkbox.

4. **State-level student privacy laws.** FERPA + COPPA isn't the full picture. SOPIPA (California), New York Ed Law 2-d, Illinois SOPPA, Colorado, Connecticut, and ~25 others impose operator obligations distinct from FERPA. The Student Privacy Pledge is table stakes for K-12 sales. Sprint 25 should explicitly enumerate which laws you're covering and where the gaps are.

5. **Observability for AI quality, not just AI cost.** Sprint 30 covers cost. Sprint 20 covers latency and failure. Neither covers *output quality* — eval harness for lesson plans, regression tests when prompts/models change, A/B framework, learner outcome metrics tied back to generation strategy. Without it, a model swap can silently degrade pedagogy and you won't know until churn shows up.

6. **Accessibility verification is named but not scoped.** "WCAG 2.2 AA" appears in Sprint 28's intro but the acceptance criteria are TTS-specific. Why the Reorder Matters

COPPA requires covered operators to obtain verifiable parental consent before collecting, using, or disclosing personal information from children under 13, so auth and consent cannot be deferred until after the learner workflows exist.

FERPA also creates student-record and disclosure-accountability expectations for education records, including maintaining records of certain disclosures of personally identifiable information from education records, so the audit and disclosure model must exist before school-facing use.

For K–12 sales, federal privacy law is not enough. California SOPIPA restricts uses of covered student information, New York Education Law 2-d protects student PII and gives parents rights regarding their child’s PII, Illinois SOPPA regulates covered student information held by schools and vendors, and Colorado’s Student Data Transparency and Security Act protects student personal information collected by state and local education providers.

SOC 2 readiness also needs its own sprint because the AICPA frames SOC 2 around controls relevant to security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy. That is not the same as “run a pen test before launch.”
